Job Description

The Position:

Zambia faces a severe, heightened risk of Ebola Bundibugyo Virus Disease (EVD) crossborder transmission due to its porous borders and high population mobility with the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC). High transmission risks extend across informal trade routes, mining corridors, and heavy transit hubs in Luapula, North-Western, Muchinga, Copperbelt, Central Provinces, and Lusaka. Key populations—including women of reproductive age, pregnant women, IDPs, and refugees in vulnerable border settlements like Mantapala—face acute exposure. Without rapid infection control and continuous health service protection, a cross-border outbreak threatens to strain local infrastructure and cause a severe breakdown in essential health services.

UNFPA's Mandatory Role and Global Mandate in Ebola Responses:

As the United Nations sexual and reproductive health agency, UNFPA plays a critical, life-saving role in public health emergencies and disease outbreaks like Ebola. Lessons from past outbreaks show that disease epidemics create a dual crisis: while the virus poses direct health threats, the collapse of routine healthcare, fear, and stigma often lead to an even sharper surge in preventable maternal and neonatal deaths. Furthermore, infectious disease outbreaks amplify gender inequalities, increase exposure risks for female primary caregivers/midwives, and elevate the risk of GenderBased Violence (GBV) and Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA).

Globally and regionally (under the Level 3 Emergency framework), UNFPA's operational role during Ebola crises focuses on five mandate-critical pillars: ● Continuity of Safe SRH & Maternal Care: Ensuring health facilities and maternity wards remain open, safe, and zero-transmission zones through the deployment of high-fluid obstetric Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), clean delivery kits, and trained midwives.

● Infection Prevention & Control (IPC) in SRH Facilities: Training health workers on outbreak-adapted clinical protocols and equipping maternal care points with specialized PPE and disinfectants.

● GBV Protection & Clinical Management of Rape (CMR): Safeguarding clinical pathways for rape survivors, delivering emergency post-violence care, maintaining One-Stop Centers (OSCs), and mitigating post-Ebola sexual transmission risks.

● Risk Communication & Community Engagement (RCCE): Mobilizing trusted community networks, faith leaders, and youth to dispel fear, combat stigma, address sexual transmission risks, and promote safe healthcare-seeking behavior.

● Protection Against Sexual Exploitation & Abuse (PSEA): Embedding accessible, confidential multi-channel reporting systems (including toll-free hotlines) and community feedback loops into national response plans.

Country Alignment & Operational Framing:

Under the Zambia National Public Health Institute (ZNPHI) Grade 2 Public Health Emergency Operations Centre (PHEOC) framework and the "One Plan, One Budget" approach, UNFPA is supporting the Ministry of Health (MoH) and ZNPHI to implement targeted preparedness interventions. Supported by the Emergency Fund (EF/HTF), UNFPA Zambia directly addresses mandate-specific gaps by integrating SRH, HIV, GBV, and PSEA into national public health structures across six high-risk provinces.

Objective

The objective of this individual national consultancy is to provide expert technical assistance to coordinate, execute, and monitor UNFPA's emergency response and preparedness interventions under the Emergency Fund (EF/HTF) allocation. The consultant will facilitate the integration of SRH and GBV preparedness into ZNPHI structures, oversee frontline health worker capacity building, coordinate subnational SRH/GBV service mapping and referral pathway adaptations, and ensure functional PSEA mechanisms.

The consultant will work under the direct supervision of UNFPA Zambia and in close coordination with MoH, ZNPHI, DMMU, Gender Division, Zambia Police (VSU), Lifeline Childline, and civil society partners. The consultant will: 1. SRH & GBV Emergency Coordination & Integration: Support UNFPA's co-leadership in the SRH and GBV Working Groups within the Grade 2 PHEOC framework and Incident Management System (IMS).

Ensure integration of maternal health, SRHR, and GBV priorities into national Incident Action Plans (IAP) and cross-border coordination mechanisms.

2. Subnational Service Mapping & Referral Pathway Adaptation: Conduct subnational SRH and GBV service mapping, facility readiness assessments, and gap analyses across six high-risk border provinces.

Update existing GBV and clinical referral pathways to integrate Ebola IPC protocols, screening procedures, survivor confidentiality, and service continuity contingencies.

3. Frontline Capacity Building & Clinical Preparedness: Coordinate competency-based trainings and simulation exercises for 200 frontline health workers (midwives, nurses, clinicians) and 50 call center staff.

Focus training content on outbreak IPC, safe deliveries during epidemics, Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), Psychological First Aid , MHPSS, RCCE, and PSEA.

Monitor the distribution of specialized high-fluid obstetric PPE, chemical disinfectants, and clinical job aids to high-risk health centers and One-Stop Centers (OSCs).

4. RCCE, AAP, and PSEA Operationalization: Facilitate ground-level RCCE outreach with Community Health Workers (CHWs), youth networks, and traditional/faith leaders, incorporating messaging on EVD sexual transmission and stigma reduction.

Monitor and synthesize data from multi-channel AAP feedback mechanisms (Lifeline Childline 116/933, DMMU 909, and lockable suggestion boxes) to close feedback loops and adapt operational responses.

Deliverables:

1. Inception Report & Detailed Emergency Workplan: Clear operational roadmap, stakeholder engagement map, and implementation timeline for the 6month preparedness response. (Due: End of Month 1)

2. SRH & GBV Subnational Gap Analysis & Adapted Referral Pathways Package: Comprehensive mapping report detailing facility readiness, commodity gaps, and finalized Ebola-adapted GBV/SRH referral pathways and SOPs across 6 high-risk provinces. (Due: End of Month 2)

3. Capacity Building & Simulation Report: Detailed completion report on the training of 200 frontline health workers and 50 call center staff, including pre/post evaluations, job aid rollouts, and simulation exercise outcomes. (Due: End of Month 4)

4. PSEA & RCCE Implementation Report: Synthesis of community feedback data, documented resolution of complaint loops, broadcast records of multimedia/podcast series, and reach metrics. (Due: End of Month 5)

5. Final Emergency Response & Transition Package: End-of-assignment package consolidating all program deliverables, indicator performance tracking (reaching 20,000 SRH and 60,000 GBV beneficiaries), and transition/sustainability recommendations for MoH/ZNPHI. (Due: End of Month 6)
