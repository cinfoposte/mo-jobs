Job Title: Programme Policy Officer - School Meals

Contract Type: International Consultant

Level: Level II

Duration: 11-months

Duty Station: Kinshasa, DRC

ORGANISATIONAL CONTEXT

The United Nations World Food Programme (WFP) is the world’s largest humanitarian organisation, saving lives in emergencies and using food assistance to build pathways to peace, stability and prosperity. In the Democratic Republic of the Congo, WFP supports government efforts to address food insecurity and malnutrition while strengthening national systems for education, social protection, resilience and food systems.

School meals contribute to school enrolment, attendance, retention, learning, nutrition and protection. They also provide a platform for supporting smallholder farmers and local economies. In line with the Government’s commitments under the School Meals Coalition, WFP provides policy advice, technical assistance, operational support, evidence generation, capacity strengthening and resource mobilisation to advance a sustainable, nationally owned school meals programme.

JOB PURPOSE

To lead the strategic development, technical oversight and operational management of WFP’s school meals portfolio in the Democratic Republic of Congo, while supporting the Government to design, finance, implement and monitor sustainable and nationally owned school meals programmes. The position is based in Kinshasa, DRC and reports to the Head of Resilience.

The position will connect field operations, and the Country Office; strengthen collaboration with government and partners; and ensure that school meals contribute to education, nutrition, social protection, resilience, gender equality and food systems transformation.

KEY ACCOUNTABILITIES

Strategic leadership and policy support

  • Serve as the Country Office technical lead and principal focal point for school meals.
  • Provide strategic and technical advice to management, field teams, government counterparts and cooperating partners.
  • Review relevant strategies, policy documents, evaluations and evidence on school meals in the DRC and the region.
  • Identify priority areas for WFP support and help operationalise the National School Feeding Strategy and School Meals Coalition commitments.
  • Ensure alignment with the WFP Country Strategic Plan, WFP policies and relevant national education, nutrition, agriculture and social protection frameworks.

Programme design and operational management

  • Lead programme design, planning, implementation, monitoring and periodic review, including targeting, beneficiary planning, food and non-food requirements, budgets, delivery arrangements and risk management.
  • Design and operationalise Home-Grown School Feeding (HGSF) models linking school meal demand with smallholder production, aggregation and local procurement, ensuring reliable supplies of safe, nutritious and diversified food.
  • Ensure interventions are evidence-based, conflict-sensitive, gender-responsive, nutrition-sensitive, inclusive and adapted to local contexts.
  • Provide technical guidance to Field and Area Offices and cooperating partners, monitor performance against plans and budgets, and initiate corrective actions where required.
  • Undertake field missions to assess programme quality, support implementation and engage schools, communities and government authorities.

Government capacity strengthening

  • Assess national and subnational capacities and develop a practical capacity-strengthening plan.
  • Provide technical assistance on policy, planning, costing, budgeting, local procurement, food safety, canteen management, monitoring, community participation and accountability.
  • Support the development of national standards, operational guidance, tools and training materials.
  • Promote a realistic transition pathway towards increased government ownership, implementation and domestic financing.

Programme integration

  • Integrate school meals with nutrition, resilience, social protection, food systems, climate adaptation, gender, protection and disability inclusion.
  • Promote Home-Grown School Feeding (HGSF) models that connect schools with smallholder farmers, producer organisations, local processors and markets.
  • Collaborate with technical units and government counterparts to promote safe, nutritious, diversified, culturally appropriate and locally sourced meals.
  • Strengthen links with national social protection systems and ensure the needs of girls, displaced children, children with disabilities and other vulnerable groups are addressed.

Coordination and partnerships

  • Maintain effective coordination among WFP Field and Area Offices, the Country Office, Regional Bureau and Headquarters.
  • Coordinate with Programme, Nutrition, Research, Assessment and Monitoring, Supply Chain, Procurement, Partnerships, Budget and Programming, Finance, Communications, Gender, Protection and Inclusion, and Technology teams.
  • Maintain strategic dialogue with the Ministry responsible for education and other relevant institutions, and clarify the role expected of WFP.
  • Reinforce collaboration with UNICEF, FAO, the World Bank, INPTA, United Nations agencies, donors, civil society, producer organisations, research institutions and the private sector.
  • Support a government-led coordination mechanism and promote harmonised standards, tools, indicators and reporting arrangements.

Monitoring, evidence and learning

  • Develop and maintain a results framework with relevant technical units and ensure timely collection, verification and use of disaggregated programme data.
  • Promote the adoption and effective use of School Connect and other relevant digital tools, including offline-capable mobile data collection, school mapping and interactive dashboards, to strengthen monitoring of attendance, meals served, food deliveries and stocks, reporting and evidence-based decision-making, in coordination with Technology teams and in line with data protection standards.
  • Monitor coverage, school feeding days, attendance, retention, girls’ participation, meal quality, local procurement, government capacity, resource use and community feedback.
  • Support assessments, evaluations, joint monitoring missions and annual portfolio reviews.
  • Produce concise knowledge products, policy briefs, case studies and lessons learned, and facilitate learning and South-South cooperation.

Resource management, mobilisation and reporting

  • Monitor financial, food and logistical resources and maintain an updated portfolio dashboard covering requirements, contributions, shortfalls, expenditure, pipeline risks and mitigation measures.
  • Ensure efficient and transparent use of resources in accordance with approved plans, budgets and WFP rules.
  • Prepare concept notes, investment cases, funding proposals, donor briefs and advocacy materials with the Partnerships and Communications teams.
  • Support the Government to develop a sustainable financing approach for school meals. Mobilize local financial and in-kind resources through partnerships with local authorities, businesses and community organizations, and promote community-based school meals models with participatory planning, transparent resource management and local ownership, ensuring that contributions remain voluntary and do not exclude vulnerable children or place undue burdens on households.
  • Prepare accurate and timely management updates, corporate reports, donor reports, briefing notes, presentations and talking points.

KEY DELIVERABLES

  • Annual school meals workplan, including priorities, responsibilities, timelines, resource requirements and risk mitigation measures.
  • Costed implementation framework for the National School Feeding Strategy and a mechanism to track School Meals Coalition commitments.
  • Costed Home-Grown School Feeding (HGSF) implementation plan linking targeted schools with smallholder farmers and producer organizations, including school food demand and supply mapping, local procurement arrangements, food safety standards, partner responsibilities, timelines and indicators to track local sourcing and smallholder participation.
  • National and subnational capacity-strengthening plan, with at least two structured government capacity-development activities each year.
  • Updated mapping of assisted schools, beneficiaries, partners and geographic coverage.
  • Monthly portfolio dashboard covering implementation, results, resources, risks and corrective actions.
  • Regularly documented internal and external coordination meetings, with decisions and actions tracked.
  • High-quality concept notes, investment cases, proposals, donor briefs and programme reports.
  • Annual portfolio review and at least one learning or knowledge product each year.
  • Communication, advocacy and visibility plan developed with the Communications Unit.

FUNCTIONAL CAPABILITIES

  • Programme lifecycle and food assistance.
  • Transfer modalities.
  • Broad knowledge of specialised areas.
  • Emergency programming.
  • Regular travel to WFP operational areas in the DRC may be required.
  • The incumbent may be required to work in challenging operating environments and support emergency response activities.
  • The incumbent must comply with WFP standards on security, safeguarding, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, accountability to affected populations, data protection, food quality and safety, and prevention of fraud and corruption.
  • Perform any other related duties assigned by the supervisor.

STANDARD MINIMUM QUALIFICATIONS

Education:

Advanced university degree in international development, public policy, education, nutrition, agriculture, food security, economics, social protection, social sciences, programme management or another field relevant to school meals; or a first university degree with additional years of relevant professional experience and training.

Experience:

  • Minimum of seven (7) years of relevant professional experience in programme and policy work related to school meals, education, nutrition, food security, social protection, resilience or food systems.
  • Experience in programme design, implementation, monitoring or evaluation in humanitarian or development settings.
  • Experience working with government institutions and coordinating multi-sectoral programmes and partnerships.
  • Experience preparing strategies, operational plans, concept notes, funding proposals and programme reports.
  • Experience with government-led school meals, home-grown school meals, local procurement, institutional capacity strengthening or resource mobilisation is desirable.
  • Experience in fragile or complex operational environments and previous experience with WFP or another United Nations organisation are assets.

Knowledge and Skills:

  • Strong understanding of programme-cycle management, results-based management, monitoring and evaluation.
  • Knowledge of school meals and their links with education, nutrition, social protection, resilience and food systems.
  • Ability to translate policy and strategic objectives into practical, costed and measurable operational plans.
  • Strong analytical, problem-solving, coordination, negotiation and stakeholder-engagement skills.
  • Excellent written and oral communication skills and ability to produce high-quality technical and strategic documents.
  • Ability to manage multiple priorities and work effectively across technical teams and field locations.
  • Leads by example with integrity.
  • Drives results and delivers on commitments.
  • Fosters inclusive and collaborative teamwork.
  • Applies strategic thinking.
  • Builds and maintains sustainable partnerships.

Languages:

Fluency (Level C) in French and English, both written and spoken, is required. Knowledge of one or more national languages of the Democratic Republic of the Congo is an advantage.
