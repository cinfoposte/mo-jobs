Background:

  1. Background and Context:

United Nations Security Council Resolution 1325 (2000) marked a turning point by formally recognizing the link between gender equality and international peace and security. Emerging from sustained advocacy by global women’s organizations, it became the foundation of the Women, Peace and Security (WPS) agenda, which has since expanded through nine subsequent resolutions.[1] Today, WPS is a core thematic pillar of the UN Security Council’s work and a key international framework addressing the gendered impacts of armed conflict, political crises, displacement, violent extremism, disasters, and mass atrocities on women and girls. The agenda promotes inclusive and democratic decision-making, gender justice, and conflict prevention, supported by evidence showing that women’s participation significantly improves peace processes and post-conflict recovery.[2] Despite progress, implementation gaps persist globally, including women’s under-representation in peace processes, limited gender-sensitive conflict analysis, and inadequate financing.

In the Philippines, these global commitments intersect with a major peace milestone in Mindanao. The 2014 Comprehensive Agreement on the Bangsamoro (CAB) between the Government of the Philippines and the Moro Islamic Liberation Front (MILF) led to the establishment of the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) and the ratification of the Bangsamoro Organic Law (BOL) in 2019. The BOL institutionalized the mainstreaming of women’s empowerment and the WPS agenda across governance and peacebuilding and created the Bangsamoro Women Commission (BWC) as the lead body for advancing these goals. BARMM’s political and fiscal autonomy offered a historic opportunity to address decades of marginalization and conflict in Southern Philippines.

However, the transition has revealed deep and persistent challenges. BARMM continues to face layered and interlinked conflicts, including clan feuds (rido), ethnic tensions affecting both Moro and non-Moro groups, political rivalries, displacement, trauma, and the threat of violent extremism. These issues extend beyond the original scope of the peace agreement and are compounded by weak inclusivity in peacebuilding processes. Gender dimensions of conflict particularly the roles, experiences, and exclusion of women—remain insufficiently addressed, despite women’s long-standing involvement in both formal and informal peace efforts.

Historically, conflict in Mindanao has reshaped gender roles in complex ways. Women have been victims, caregivers, economic providers, peace advocates, and at times combatants or supporters within armed structures, while men have experienced shifting notions of masculinity linked to loss of livelihoods and exposure to violence. Although women have often filled critical social and economic gaps during displacement and insecurity, they remain largely excluded from formal peace and security decision-making. Political representation of women in BARMM institutions remains below 10 per cent, significantly lower than the national average. These persistent inequalities continue to shape women’s experiences of conflict and peacebuilding today and underscore the importance of institutionalizing the Women, Peace and Security (WPS) agenda throughout BARMM’s ongoing political transition.

The successful conduct and completion of the first Bangsamoro Parliamentary Elections (BPE) in September 2026 marked a significant milestone in the political transition of the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM). The inauguration of the first regular BARMM Government on 30 October 2026 signals the beginning of a new phase of democratic governance and institution-building in the region. While this transition presents important opportunities to deepen inclusive and accountable governance, it also underscores the urgency of sustaining and institutionalizing gains in women's participation, leadership, and protection within emerging governance structures. Notably, the Bangsamoro Gender and Development (GAD) Code has yet to be enacted, leaving critical gaps in the legal and policy framework needed to advance gender equality and women's empowerment across BARMM institutions. In this context, the integration of Women, Peace and Security (WPS) principles into government policies, programmes, and budgets remains more critical than ever. The BARMM Regional Action Plan on Women, Peace and Security (RAP-WPS) 2023–2028, coordinated by the Bangsamoro Women Commission (BWC), continues to provide the principal framework for translating policy commitments into concrete and adequately funded actions. At the same time, peacebuilding and development efforts continue to face challenges arising from displacement, localized conflicts and rido, resource competition, shrinking civic space, environmental degradation, climate-induced disasters, and regional geopolitical developments that disproportionately affect women and girls.

Against this backdrop, the Joint Programme (JP) has been implemented since April 2022 during a critical transition period marked by political, social, and environmental complexities. Following the completion of the first Bangsamoro Parliamentary Elections and the establishment of the regular BARMM Government, the region continues to face persistent peace and security challenges, including localized conflicts, clan feuds (rido), violent extremism risks, and political competition. These challenges are compounded by recurring climate- and disaster-related shocks, including severe flooding and sea-level rise, which exacerbate displacement, livelihood losses, and gender-based violence. Women and girls continue to face intersecting vulnerabilities arising from conflict, disasters, climate change, and persistent economic exclusion, while Non-Moro Indigenous Peoples, particularly women, remain among the most marginalized groups, often excluded from formal peace and governance processes and disproportionately affected by localized conflicts and land disputes.

Despite these challenges, important policy and political openings continue to provide opportunities to advance the Women, Peace and Security (WPS) agenda in BARMM. The successful completion of the first Bangsamoro Parliamentary Elections in September 2026 and the inauguration of the first regular BARMM Government on 30 October 2026 mark a historic transition towards democratic self-governance. However, the transition period concluded without the enactment of the Bangsamoro Gender and Development (GAD) Code, representing a significant gap in the institutionalization of gender equality and women's empowerment within the Bangsamoro legal framework. This underscores the importance of strengthening the implementation of existing gender-responsive policies and accelerating efforts to establish a comprehensive legislative foundation for women's rights and participation. At the same time, existing gender provisions in the Bangsamoro Electoral Code and Bangsamoro Local Governance Code, growing national momentum for the localization of the National Action Plan on Women, Peace and Security (NAP-WPS), and renewed global commitments under the Pasay Declaration on WPS, which highlights the interlinkages between security, climate change, and gender equality, provide critical entry points for advancing the WPS agenda. The 2024–2028 United Nations Sustainable Development Cooperation Framework (UNSDCF) further reinforces these priorities by committing the UN system to inclusive peacebuilding and ensuring that no one is left behind, particularly marginalized women, Indigenous women, former combatants, youth, and those living in geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas.

Building on these enabling policy and institutional frameworks, the JP has played a catalytic role in advancing the localization of the WPS agenda. Most notably, the JP supported the development and launch of the BARMM Regional Action Plan on Women, Peace and Security (RAP-WPS) 2023–2028 in October 2023 and subsequently assisted in its early implementation and localization. The BARMM RAP-WPS has emerged as a landmark example of localized WPS planning in the Philippines and is recognized in the NAP-WPS 2023–2033 as a “unique document” that offers valuable lessons for adapting national WPS commitments to local conflict and peacebuilding realities.

While these policy gains have created a strong foundation for advancing women’s participation and leadership in peacebuilding, sustaining momentum requires continued investment and support. In response, an 18‑month extension supported through a top-up contribution from the Government of Canada has enabled the JP to consolidate gains and respond to emerging risks by:

  • Deepening the localization of WPS through coordinated work with Office of the Presidential Adviser on Peace, Reconciliation and Unity (OPAPRU), Philippine Commission on Women (PCW), national agencies, local government units, and civil society;
  • Supporting women’s political leadership and participation at different levels of governance;
  • Advancing women’s economic empowerment at the intersection of peacebuilding and climate resilience;
  • Strengthening the capacity and accompaniment of women peacebuilders and mediators, including those from Moro, Non-Moro Indigenous Peoples, settler, and youth communities;
  • Expanding programming to the Special Geographic Areas in North Cotabato and BARMM-contiguous areas; and
  • Extending WPS localization efforts to other priority regions, including the Cordillera Administrative Region and the Caraga Region.

By building institutional capacity, empowering women leaders, and grounding the WPS agenda in local realities, the JP positions women not as passive victims of conflict, but as central agents of peace. The implementation of the JP has been critical to ensuring that the gains of the peace process translate into inclusive, resilient, and sustainable peace in BARMM and beyond.

  1. Alignment with Development Priorities of GAC:

With funding support from the Global Affairs Canada (GAC), this programme aligns with GAC Feminist International Assistance Policy, more specifically ‘Action area 1 (core): Gender equality and empowerment of women and girls’ and ‘Action area 6: Peace and security’. This programme is grounded on global evidence which says that societies that are more gender equal are more peaceful. Recognizing that when women are involved in peace and security efforts, solutions are more effective and longer lasting, this programme seeks to integrate women and women’s needs into BARMM’s regional peace and security processes. It seeks to leverage more effective and productive partnerships, bringing together BARMM ministries and agencies with civil society, international organizations, and academia. The programme draws inspiration from GAC’s global efforts to strengthen the WPS agenda, including through the Group of Friends on WPS and Canada’s National Action Plan (NAP) WPS. This programme also directly aligns with the UN Sustainable Development Framework (UNSDCF) Outcome 1[3], Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) 5 and 16, as well as Canada’s NAP WPS in the following objective areas:

  • Increasing the meaningful participation of women, women’s organizations and networks in conflict prevention, conflict resolution, and post-conflict state building.
  • Preventing and responding to sexual and gender-based violence perpetrated in conflict and sexual exploitation and abuse by peacekeepers
  • Promoting and protecting women’s and girls’ human rights, gender equality and the empowerment of women and girls in fragile, conflict and post-conflict settings.
  • Strengthening the capacity of peace operations to advance the WPS agenda
  1. Programme Description:

The Empowering Women for Sustainable Peace in the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) and the Philippines is a joint initiative of UN Women and UNDP designed to prevent insecurity and violence in BARMM by addressing structural gender inequalities and discriminatory social norms that undermine sustainable peace. The Joint Programme (JP) was initially implemented in BARMM and was later expanded to the national level through a costed top-up, particularly in the Caraga Region and the Cordillera Administrative Region (CAR).

The JP commenced in April 2022 and was originally scheduled to end in September 2025. However, through the costed top-up, the programme was extended until March 2027. The JP is supported by the Government of Canada with a total budget of CAD 4.5 million, which includes the CAD 1.5 million costed extension.

There have been no major changes to the Performance Monitoring Framework (PMF) at either the immediate or intermediate outcome levels. However, additional indicators and targets were introduced to measure results in the top-up areas. For the complete PMF of the JP, please refer to the annexes and other relevant project documents.

The JP has focused on two mutually reinforcing priorities:

  1. Strengthening women’s leadership and participation in peacebuilding, mediation, conflict prevention, and resolution at community and institutional levels; and
  2. Enhancing the capacity, ownership, and accountability of BARMM ministries, offices, and agencies to mainstream gender equality and the WPS agenda in policies, plans, and programmes.

The JP ultimate outcome is by 2028, all people, especially those at risk of being left behind, have increased resilience to economic, climatic, disaster, and public health risk through improved, equitable, and gender-responsive access to and utilization of quality social services, social protection, healthy habitat, and enhanced good governance and peace which is the outcome 1 of the new 2024-2028 UNSDCF Philippines framework.

The JP has two intermediate outcomes:

Intermediate Outcomes 1100: Strengthened inclusive, gender-responsive policy and programming on conflict prevention and resolution by government institutions in the BARMM and the Philippines. (GAC: 1100)

(Equivalent to the UN outcome: Government institutions in the BARMM strengthen inclusive, gender-responsive policy and programming on conflict prevention and resolution)

Intermediate Outcomes 1200: Improved meaningful participation and leadership in conflict prevention and resolution and peace-humanitarian nexus of women peace mediators and women’s organizations in the BARMM and the Philippines ((GAC: 1200) (Equivalent to the UN outcome: Women peace mediators and women’s organizations have meaningful participation and leadership in conflict prevention for sustaining peace and address a peace-humanitarian nexus in BARMM)

  1. Expected Results and beneficiaries:

The JP has continued the implementation of the WPS agenda into other regions and national level interventions. From the original JP results framework, the top-up will contribute to the original intermediate outcomes which still focus on BARMM but also extends support towards national WPS priorities, including NAP-WPS localization to other regions.

The primary beneficiaries of the joint programme include women and girls in BARMM, particularly those who are vulnerable to violence, conflict and crisis in the region. The immediate beneficiaries include the Bangsamoro Transition Authority (BTA), BWC, BARMM ministries and National Agencies (duty bearers) and gender advocates, civil society women’s organizations and associations at regional and national levels (rights holders).

The Joint Programme is guided by the following theory of change:

If Government institutions in the BARMM and the Philippines strengthen inclusive, gender-responsive policy and programming on conflict prevention and resolution; and

Women peace mediators and women’s organizations have meaningful participation and leadership in conflict prevention for sustaining peace and address a peace-humanitarian nexus in BARMM and in the Philippines;

Then the contribution of women and girls to peacebuilding and consolidation of peace will be valued and bring broader benefits to communities in BARMM and in the Philippines; because inclusive governance and peacebuilding processes promote just and lasting peace in conflict-affected areas of Mindanao and the country.

  1. Objectives and use of the final evaluation:

The overall purpose of the evaluation is to assess the relevance, effectiveness, efficiency, coherence, sustainability, and emerging impact of the Joint Programme, while generating strategic evidence to inform future decision-making, strengthen accountability, and promote organizational learning among stakeholders.

The key objectives of the evaluation are to:

  1. Assess the JP’s performance against the agreed evaluation criteria and its progress toward expected results;
  2. Examine the integration of gender equality and human rights in the JP implementation, including assessing the extent to which Mid-Term Review findings informed the remaining implementation period;
  3. Document the JP’s contribution to higher-level and transformative results, including intended and unintended changes and sustainability of results;
  4. Collect and validate stories of change, lessons learned, good practices, and innovative approaches; and
  5. Provide actionable recommendations for future WPS programming, replication, and scale-up.

The evaluation will apply the OECD-DAC criteria and an additional criterion on gender equality and human rights, in line with the commitments of the Government of Canada, UN Women, and UNDP.

  1. Scope:

The evaluation will focus primarily on the 2022 (Inception stage)–2027 (first Quarter) implementation period of the project, with a view toward identifying lessons learned and actionable recommendations for future programming. The evaluation will assess the JP’s inputs, activities, outputs, use of outputs, and progress toward immediate outcomes, including its contribution to higher-level results where evidence is available. It will examine the extent to which the programme has contributed to advancing the Women, Peace and Security agenda through institutional strengthening, policy support, capacity development, and engagement with target groups and communities.

The evaluation will also examine the extent to which recommendations from the 2024 Mid-Term Review were implemented and used to adjust the programme’s course of action.

The evaluation will cover all geographic areas where the JP has been implemented and will consider results across relevant levels of intervention, including national, BARMM, CAR, CARAGA local government, institutional, and community levels. Particular attention will be given to the experiences, participation, and benefits of target groups[1], in line with the JP’s intended results and accountability commitments. The identification of field sites should be determine by the lead evaluator in consultation with the JP team.

  1. Design, Process and Methodology:

The final evaluation will adopt a utilization-focused, theory-based, mixed-methods, participatory, and gender-responsive approach. It will ensure that the perspectives of government partners, duty-bearers, rights-holders, women-led organizations, and community beneficiaries are reflected in the assessment.

The evaluation will use multiple lines of evidence and triangulate findings from quantitative and qualitative data sources to strengthen the validity and credibility of conclusions. It will assess progress against the JP theory of change and results framework, examining how JP interventions contributed to changes at national, BARMM, institutional, local government, and community levels.

The evaluation will build on existing evidence generated throughout the programme lifecycle, including the 2022 baseline study, the 2024 Mid-Term Review, project monitoring data, and available sex-, age-, and diversity-disaggregated data. These sources will be complemented by primary data collection and stakeholder consultations to validate findings, assess results achieved, identify lessons learned, and generate recommendations for future Women, Peace and Security programming.

The evaluation is expected to include the following methodological components:

  • Theory of Change and evaluability assessment: The evaluator will review the existing JP Theory of Change (ToC), results framework, and assumptions to assess their validity and determine how JP interventions contributed to observed results. Where necessary, the ToC may be refined to reflect contextual or programmatic changes that occurred during implementation.
  • Desk review and secondary data analysis: The evaluation will review relevant JP documentation, including the project document, annual work plans, baseline study (2022), Mid-Term Review (2024), monitoring and reporting data, evaluations, knowledge products, and other relevant documents. Existing sex-, age-, and diversity-disaggregated data will be analyzed to assess progress and results.
  • Primary data collection: A combination of qualitative and quantitative methods will be used to collect and triangulate evidence. Methods may include:
    • Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) with UN Women, UNDP, government counterparts, implementing partners, donors, and other stakeholders;
    • Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) with rights-holders, community beneficiaries, women-led organizations, and other target groups;
    • Surveys, where appropriate, to gather broader stakeholder perspectives and validate key findings; and
    • Most Significant Change (MSC) and storytelling techniques to capture perceived changes, unintended results, and transformative impacts experienced by JP participants.
  • Stakeholder engagement: The evaluation will engage a diverse range of stakeholders, including UN Women and UNDP programme teams, government institutions, local government units, civil society organizations, implementing partners, rights-holders, and community beneficiaries, ensuring the meaningful participation of women and marginalized groups.
  • Sampling strategy: The evaluator will develop a sampling framework that ensures adequate representation of stakeholder groups, intervention areas, and vulnerable populations. Site selection for field visits should be transparent, purposive, and representative of the geographic and programmatic diversity of the JP. The lead evaluator is independently determine the sample and the field sites in consultation with the JP team.
  • Data analysis and triangulation: Findings will be generated through systematic triangulation of information from multiple sources and methods. The analysis will assess programme performance against the evaluation criteria and answer the evaluation questions while considering gender equality, human rights, conflict sensitivity, and inclusion dimensions.
  • Inception phase: The final methodological approach, including the evaluation matrix, sampling strategy, data sources, data collection schedule, analytical framework, and quality assurance measures, shall be detailed in the Inception Report and reviewed and agreed upon with the Evaluation Manager and key evaluation stakeholders prior to data collection.
  • Fieldwork and onsite participation: The evaluation team is expected to conduct onsite field visits to selected JP locations to undertake stakeholder consultations, KIIs, FGDs, and direct observation where necessary to validate findings and capture contextual factors influencing JP results.

The evaluation team and its collaborators will follow the UN Women Evaluation Handbook, UNDP Evaluation Guidelines, and UNEG Norms and Standards for Evaluation. The process will uphold independence, impartiality, ethical conduct, inclusivity, conflict sensitivity, and the use of credible evidence for accountability, learning, and decision-making.

The evaluation is expected to report using disaggregated data. To the extent possible, the evaluation will integrate an inclusive approach and ensure do no harm in engaging marginalized groups, including from diverse SOGIESC, persons with disabilities and/or ethnic minorities, data privacy guidelines. Ethical protocol will be developed and annexed to the inception report.

The evaluation findings will be disseminated through a structured knowledge-sharing and utilization process targeting primary and secondary users. This will include a validation workshop with the Joint Project Steering Committee and key stakeholders, dissemination of an executive summary and policy brief, learning and reflection sessions with implementing partners to ensure accountability to rights-holders and beneficiaries. The findings and recommendations will further inform management responses, future Women, Peace and Security programming, policy dialogue, and the potential scaling and replication of successful approaches within BARMM and other regions of the Philippines.

The evaluation’s value added is its impartial and systematic assessment of the project. As with the other stages of the evaluation, involvement of stakeholders should not interfere with the impartiality of the evaluation. The evaluators have the final judgment on the findings, conclusions and recommendations of the evaluation report, and the evaluator must be protected from pressures to change information in the report.

Proper procedures for data collection with rights holders who may have been affected by violence must be adhered to as outlined in the Improving the collection and use of administrative data on VAW and WHO Ethical and Safety Recommendations for research on violence against women. Fieldwork shall comply with applicable UN security requirements, and data collection should be conducted in appropriate local languages and, where relevant, by women data collectors. Enhanced safeguarding measures shall apply to participants under 18. Particular attention shall be given to protecting the identity, confidentiality and security of women mediators, women peacebuilders and other potentially at-risk participants, including through anonymization or aggregation of sensitive information where necessary. These safeguards, including data protection and secure storage arrangements, shall be detailed in the inception report and applied throughout data collection, analysis and reporting. Additionally, if the evaluator(s) identify issues of wrongdoing, fraud or other unethical conduct, UN Women and UNDP procedures must be followed and confidentiality be maintained. The UN Women Legal Framework for Addressing Non-Compliance withUN Standards of Conduct, and accompanying policies protecting against retaliation and prohibiting harassment and abuse of authority, provide a cohesive framework aimed at creating and maintaining a harmonious working environment, ensuring that staff members do not engage in any wrongdoing and that all allegations of wrongdoing are reported promptly, investigated and appropriate action taken to achieve accountability.

The Lead Evaluator and evaluation team members shall maintain independence and impartiality throughout the evaluation. They must disclose any actual, potential, or perceived conflict of interest, including prior or current professional, financial, organizational, or other relationships with the JP, participating UN organizations, implementing partners, donors, or other key stakeholders that could compromise, or reasonably be perceived to compromise, the independence of the evaluation. Any conflict identified before or during the evaluation shall be promptly disclosed and addressed through appropriate mitigation measures.

The evaluation team may use approved AI tools to support specific evaluation tasks, provided their use complies with UN Women and UNDP requirements on data protection, information security, ethics and safeguarding. Any sensitive data used in AI-supported processing must be appropriately protected and anonymized, and AI-generated outputs must be reviewed and validated by the evaluation team. AI may support but will not replace human evaluative judgement, and the evaluation team remains fully responsible for the analysis, findings, conclusions and recommendations.

  1. Management Arrangements:

JPSC as the Evaluation Reference Group

The Joint Project Steering Committee (JPSC), serving as the Evaluation Reference Group (ERG), will provide strategic oversight, institutional guidance, and governance support throughout the evaluation process. The JPSC will help ensure that the evaluation remains relevant to the Joint Programme’s strategic objectives, stakeholder priorities, and accountability and learning needs, while safeguarding the independence, credibility, and impartiality of the evaluation.

In its ERG capacity, the JPSC will provide strategic guidance on the evaluation scope and Terms of Reference; facilitate meaningful engagement of key stakeholders; review and provide strategic inputs on major evaluation deliverables; and support the validation and contextualization of evaluation findings and recommendations. The JPSC will also promote ownership, learning, and use of evaluation results in programme decision-making and future programming. Following completion of the evaluation, the JPSC will provide strategic oversight to the development and endorsement of the management response and monitor progress on agreed follow-up actions arising from the evaluation recommendations. While exercising these functions, the JPSC will maintain an oversight and advisory role and will not interfere with the evaluators’ methodological decisions, analysis, judgments, or conclusions, thereby preserving the independence and integrity of the evaluation process.

The evaluation will be jointly managed by UN Women and UNDP, with clearly defined roles and accountabilities throughout the evaluation process. Overall management responsibility will rest with the UN Women WPS Programme Specialist, supported by the Monitoring and Reporting Analyst for day-to-day coordination, documentation, stakeholder engagement, and consolidation of comments from the Joint Programme (JP) team. The Lead Evaluator will have primary responsibility for the technical leadership and conduct of the evaluation, including the evaluation design and methodology, data collection and analysis, and preparation of evidence-based findings, conclusions, recommendations, and all evaluation deliverables. The Lead Evaluator will retain independent professional judgment and responsibility for determining how comments are addressed and incorporated, consistent with applicable evaluation standards.

The review and approval of key evaluation deliverables, including the inception report, draft evaluation report, and final evaluation report, will follow a clear sequence: (1) Lead Evaluator submission, with the Lead Evaluator submitting the deliverable to the evaluation management team; (2) JP Team review, with UN Women and UNDP programme teams reviewing the deliverable for factual accuracy, completeness, programme context, and consistency with the agreed scope, and consolidating comments for the Lead Evaluator; (3) Technical Quality Assurance, with the UN Women ROAP IES and UNDP Results and Quality Team reviewing the deliverable for methodological rigor, evidence-based analysis, and compliance with applicable UNEG, UN Women, GERAAS, and UNDP evaluation standards; (4) Lead Evaluator revision and finalization, with the Lead Evaluator considering and addressing consolidated comments while retaining independence over evaluation judgments, findings, conclusions, and recommendations; (5) ERG/JPSC strategic review and acceptance, with the JPSC, acting as the Evaluation Reference Group (ERG), reviewing the deliverable from a strategic governance, accountability, learning, and stakeholder perspective and confirming that the agreed evaluation scope and requirements have been satisfactorily addressed; and (6) Formal approval, with the designated evaluation management authority confirming completion and approval of the deliverable following technical quality assurance and JPSC/ERG acceptance. Formal acceptance or approval of a deliverable will confirm that the agreed contractual and evaluation requirements have been met and will not constitute approval, alteration, or endorsement of the independent findings, conclusions, or recommendations, which remain the professional responsibility of the Lead Evaluator.

The Evaluation lead National Consultant will be supported by two Data Collector (National Consultant) that will facilitate data collection processes, such as collection of documents and co-facilitation of interviews and focus group discussions. The Data Collector National Consultant will directly report to the Monitoring and Reporting Analyst hired under the Joint Programme and the evaluation lead provides the technical direction of the two data collectors work/task. The Evaluation Lead National Consultant will report to the WPS Programme Specialist of UN Women Philippines, in close collaboration with the UNDP Peacebuilding Project Manager-Peace and Stabilization Team. Both consultants are expected to work closely in the conduct of this final evaluation, and to undertake regular consultations and updating with the UN Women and UNDP JP team. The authority of approval of all the deliverable will be via joint UN Women and UNDP consent. UN Women administrative approach will be strictly followed throughout the review process.
