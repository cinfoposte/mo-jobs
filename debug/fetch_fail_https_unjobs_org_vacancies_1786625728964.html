Background/Context:

UN Women leads the UN system's efforts to advance gender equality and women's empowerment. The UN Women Kyrgyz Republic Country Office Strategic Note (2023-2027) aligns with national priorities and international commitments on gender equality, ending violence against women, women's economic empowerment, and the Women, Peace and Security agenda.

This Country Portfolio Evaluation (CPE) will assess the relevance, coherence, effectiveness, efficiency, sustainability, and human rights and gender equality dimensions of the Strategic Note to support learning, accountability, and evidence-based decision-making for the next programming cycle.

Implementation of the Strategic Note took place in a complex and evolving political, socio-economic, and civic environment. Despite progress in strengthening the legal and policy framework on gender equality and women's empowerment (GEWE), challenges such as shrinking civic space, political and economic instability, gender inequalities, and violence against women and girls continued to affect vulnerable groups, particularly women with disabilities, women living with HIV, female-headed households, and women in remote areas. In response, the Country Office adapted its partnerships and policy engagement approaches to remain responsive to emerging challenges.

The evaluation will assess how effectively the Strategic Note addressed interconnected barriers to GEWE and aligned national priorities with international frameworks, including CEDAW, the Beijing Platform for Action, UNSCR 1325, and the 2030 Agenda. It is guided by UN Women and UNEG Norms and Standards (2016), the 2024 UNEG Guidance on Integrating Human Rights and Gender Equality in Evaluations, the UNEG Ethical Guidelines (2020), and the UN Women Evaluation Policy and Handbook, while observing the principles of integrity, accountability, respect, and beneficence.

UN WOMEN KYRGYZ REPUBLIC'S STRATEGIC NOTE (SN) 2023-2027

SN Theory of Change The UN Women Strategic Note for the Kyrgyz Republic is based on the premise that transformative progress in gender equality and women's empowerment (GEWE) can only be achieved when women and girls, especially those furthest behind are meaningfully included in governance, economic development, peacebuilding, and decision-making processes. It recognizes that linear and one-size-fits-all approaches are insufficient in a context marked by political volatility, shrinking civic space, socio-economic pressures, climate risks, and deeply entrenched gender norms. Therefore, change is expected through coordinated action among state institutions, local authorities, civil society, the private sector, and the UN system to strengthen accountability, scale locally grounded solutions, and address the root causes of inequality.

The Strategic Note assumes that accelerated GEWE outcomes will emerge when: (i) institutions have the capacity, incentives, and tools to implement human rights-based, survivor-centered, and gender-responsive policies and services; (ii) women, youth, and intersectional rights groups are empowered as active participants and solution holders in political, peacebuilding, and economic processes; and (iii) harmful social norms are systematically challenged through education, media, community engagement, and the involvement of men and boys. Its programmatic logic is built on four interconnected pillars: governance and participation, women's economic empowerment, elimination of violence against women and girls (EVAWG), and Women, Peace and Security (WPS). These areas are designed to reinforce one another and generate systemic rather than isolated results. Through approaches such as adaptive leadership, design thinking, inclusive consultations, and alignment with international frameworks including CEDAW, BPfA, UNSCR 1325, SDGs, and UPR recommendations, the Strategic Note aims to strengthen institutional coherence, social trust, inclusive growth, and sustainable peace. By 2027, it seeks to ensure that women and girls, particularly those facing multiple forms of exclusion more equally enjoy their rights, agency, opportunities, and security in a resilient and violence-free environment.

UN Women's work in Kyrgyzstan is implemented through its triple mandate:

  • Normative mandate-supporting the Government in implementing and monitoring international and national commitments on gender equality, including CEDAW, the Beijing Platform for Action, UNSCR 1325, the National Gender Equality Strategy until 2030, and gender-responsive legislation and policies related to EVAWG, women's leadership, budgeting, climate resilience, and peacebuilding.
  • Coordination mandate- leading and supporting UN system-wide coordination and accountability on GEWE. The Country Office chairs and coordinates key inter-agency mechanisms, including the UNCT Gender Thematic Group (GTG), UNCT-SWAP Gender Scorecard, GEAP implementation, and from mid-2026 the UN Working Group on LNOB. UN Women plays a central role in integrating gender commitments across the UNSDCF Results Groups, co-chairing RG2 (2023-2024) and RG4 (since 2025), while contributing to joint advocacy, resource mobilization, and system-wide coherence through multiple UN coordination platforms.
  • Operational mandate - supporting implementation through partnerships with government institutions, Parliament, civil society organizations, and development partners via consultations, policy dialogue, advocacy, and programmatic interventions focused on women's leadership, economic empowerment, EVAWG, and peacebuilding.

PURPOSE, OBJECTIVES AND USE OF THE EVALUATION

The evaluation aims to:

  • Support evidence-based learning and decision-making for UN Women Kyrgyz Republic Country Office.
  • Ensure accountability for contributions to gender equality and women's empowerment.
  • Capture lessons learned and good practices to inform the next Strategic Note.

    The primary users of this evaluation include the UN Women Kyrgyz Republic Country Office, the Regional Office for Europe and Central Asia, and UN Women Headquarters, as well as national government institutions, civil society and youth organizations, private sector partners, donors, development partners, the United Nations Country Team, the Gender Theme Group (GTG), and programme beneficiaries, including target groups, their households, and communities.

The objectives of the evaluation are to:

  • Assess the relevance and alignment of the UN Women Kyrgyz Republic Country Office Strategic Note with national priorities and international commitments on gender equality and women's empowerment.
  • Evaluate the effectiveness, organizational efficiency, and strategic positioning of the Strategic Note in advancing gender equality and women's empowerment in the Kyrgyz Republic.
  • Assess the integration of human rights and gender equality principles across the design and implementation of the Strategic Note.
  • Identify lessons learned, good practices, innovations, and synergies across UN Women's normative, coordination, and operational mandates.
  • Provide actionable recommendations to inform the development of the next Strategic Note.

Specifically, the CPE will be used for:

  • Supporting learning and evidence-based decision-making for the development of the next UN Women Kyrgyz Republic Country Office Strategic Note (2028-2032).
  • Ensuring accountability for the results and contributions of the Strategic Note 2023-2027 to gender equality and women's empowerment.
  • Strengthening the capacity and engagement of national stakeholders in advancing gender equality and women's empowerment.

EVALUATION CRITERIA AND KEY QUESTIONS

The evaluation will apply the five OECD/DAC evaluation criteria (relevance, coherence, effectiveness, efficiency, and sustainability), as well as Human Rights and Gender Equality as an additional criterion. The evaluation will seek to answer the following key evaluation questions and sub-questions: Key Questions:

1. Relevance: How aligned are the interventions with Kyrgyz Republic's national policies and international frameworks?

2. Coherence: To what extent do the interventions complement efforts by other actors?

3. Effectiveness: What results have been achieved, and what factors have influenced these outcomes? 4. Efficiency: How effectively have resources been utilized?

5. Sustainability: Are interventions designed to ensure lasting impact?

6. Human Rights and Gender Equality: How well does the portfolio address the underlying causes of gender inequality?

DESCRIPTION OF RESPONSIBILITY/ SCOPE OF WORK

The evaluation will cover the 2023-2026 implementation period and, where relevant, consider the strategic and operational directions reflected in the 2026-2027 biannual workplans. It will assess all activities implemented under the Strategic Note, including normative, coordination, and programmatic work aligned with the UN Women Strategic Plan 2022-2025. Joint programs and initiatives are also within scope, including assessment of UN Women's contribution and the added value of joint modalities.

As many program activities are implemented at the local level, the evaluation team may conduct field visits in selected locations in the Kyrgyz Republic, subject to security and feasibility considerations. While the evaluation does not cover operations in Tajikistan, it may review the Kyrgyz Republic Country Office's operational support role as part of the assessment of efficiency and effectiveness.

During the inception phase, the evaluation team will undertake a rapid evaluability assessment, including review of the theory of change, performance indicators in the Development Result Framework (DRF) and Organizationl effectiveness and efficiency (OEE) data availability, evaluation context, and accountability structures. The Country Office's initial assessment indicates high availability of relevant secondary data, including monitoring, financial, management, and communication records.

EVALUATION DESIGN AND METHODOLOGY

UN Women evaluations apply a gender-responsive and human rights-based approach that is inclusive, participatory, transparent, and focused on addressing structural barriers and harmful social norms affecting women's rights. The evaluation uses theory-based, utilization-focused, and feminist approaches to assess power relations, inequalities, inclusion, and transformative change, while ensuring stakeholder participation throughout the process.

The evaluation follows UNEG Norms and Standards (2016), ethical guidelines (2020), and UN Women Evaluation Policy and Handbook. It builds on relevant evaluations conducted during the Strategic Note period and applies a theory-based design aligned with the Strategic Note 2023-2027.

The assessment combines portfolio analysis, desk review, stakeholder consultations, and contribution analysis, using mixed qualitative and quantitative methods. Data collection tools include interviews, focus groups, document analysis, and observation, with strong attention to data quality, triangulation, confidentiality, and inclusion of vulnerable groups.

The evaluation also examines risks, learning opportunities, scalability, and the strategic relevance of interventions, while considering the country's cultural, social, economic, and institutional context.

STAKEHOLDER PARTICIPATION

The Evaluation Reference Group (ERG) will support quality assurance, stakeholder engagement, methodological reflection, factual validation, and the use of evaluation findings.

The evaluation will ensure meaningful participation of key stakeholders throughout the process, with particular attention to rights holders and vulnerable groups, while considering practical limitations. Stakeholders include target groups, government institutions, programme partners, civil society, private sector actors, donors, the UN system, and other relevant actors.

Stakeholder analysis should consider institutional roles, gender and social dimensions, human rights perspectives, and intended evaluation users. The evaluation should apply a gender-responsive and rights-based approach, assess relationships and power dynamics, and validate findings through consultations and debriefings. Final stakeholder inclusion and evaluation boundaries will be agreed during the inception phase.

EVALUATION GOVERNANCE

All UN Women evaluations apply established quality assurance mechanisms in line with the UN Women Evaluation Policy and Evaluation Manual. The Strategic Note Evaluation report will follow UN Women reporting standards and the UN Writing Manual, with quality assessed through the GERAAS framework. The evaluation will follow the IES Guidance Note on Short Evaluation Reports (2026). The main report should normally not exceed 7,000 words, excluding annexes, with full evidence and technical detail retained in annexes (assessed for quality but not counted towards the word limit). All GERAAS criteria continue to apply, and recommendations will be limited to a prioritized set of typically three to five actions. All deliverables will undergo review by peer reviewers, the Evaluation Reference Group (ERG), and the Evaluation Management Group (EMG).
