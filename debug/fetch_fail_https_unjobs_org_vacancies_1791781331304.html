Background:Egypt is currently facing a strained economic environment driven by regional instability and global trade disruptions. The Middle East crisis that erupted in February 2026 has intensified pressures on the economy, raising oil prices and import costs and adding further stress to Egypt’s external position. As a net importer of strategic crops such as wheat, as well as oil and natural gas — the latter being a critical input for fertilizer production — Egypt remains highly exposed to global price volatility.After notable improvements in 2025, when the Egyptian pound appreciated from EGP 50.4 in January to EGP 47.6 in December and inflation eased significantly — with overall inflation falling from 23.2 percent in January to 10.3 percent by December, and food inflation dropping from 20.2 percent to just 0.9 percent — the February 2026 crisis triggered a depreciation to EGP 53 in May 2026. This reversal reignited inflationary pressures across both food and non food items. Rising costs have strained household budgets and forced many families to adopt negative coping strategies, including reducing meal sizes, lowering dietary diversity, and shifting to cheaper, less nutritious foods. These behaviors have serious implications for food security and nutritional status, particularly among vulnerable groups such as pregnant and breastfeeding women, infants and young children, the elderly, and households already living in poverty.In response, the government has sought to balance fiscal pressures with measures to protect household purchasing power and access to basic needs. Government actions have included increases in fuel, electricity, and railway ticket prices, alongside consideration of shifting food subsidies to cash transfers and selling bread at market prices. At the same time, the government has encouraged local wheat production to reduce import dependence and trade imbalances. Complementary measures have included raising the minimum wage and the rollout of a temporary expansion of Egypt’s social protection scheme. These interventions aim to cushion households against the irreversible consequences of poverty, food insecurity, and malnutrition.Against this backdrop, the objective of the consultancy is to generate evidence on how recent economic shocks and policy measures affect household purchasing power, food security, and nutrition. The consultant will leverage the most recent Household Income and Expenditure Surveys (HIECS) and previous datasets to estimate the prevalence of food insecurity and nutrition deficiencies, analyze trends over time, and profile food insecure households by socioeconomic, geographic, and dwelling characteristics.Building on this analysis, the consultant will develop a concise nutrition synthesis using HIECS data and relevant nutrition indicators to examine underlying drivers, correlations, disparities, and trends where possible. This will strengthen the interpretation of how economic shocks, food insecurity, poverty, and policy measures translate into dietary and nutrition risks for vulnerable groups, particularly women, children, and households enrolled in social protection programmes, specifically under the ‘ First 1000 days’ programme, and also food subsidy schemes in line with updated reference food basket for Egyptians. The findings will also inform the Cost of Diet and Cost of Food and Nutrition studies, supporting evidence-based programming and policy dialogue under the national food systems agenda.The consultant will simulate different scenarios for government measures — including subsidy reforms and social assistance expansions — to assess their potential impact on household vulnerability, food insecurity, dietary patterns, and nutrition outcomes. Building on these models, the consultant will deliver strategic, programmatic, and policy insights grounded in evidence. This will enable proactive and timely interventions to mitigate the socio economic impacts on household food security and nutrition, particularly among the most vulnerable. Evidence generation will also focus on identifying the groups most at risk and their specific food security and nutrition needs, thereby informing recommendations for the Government of Egypt on the most appropriate interventions. This aligns with government priorities under the food systems approach, consistent with Egypt’s commitments to the Food Systems Summit resolutions of 2021 and national initiatives — including the National Operational Plan for Food and Nutrition Systems 2025–2030 and the Accelerated Anemia Action Plan.Furthermore, the analysis will explore the degree to which food insecurity intersects with poverty and assesses the impact of single and multi layered social protection and subsidy interventions in addressing household vulnerabilities. It will also provide evidence on how existing government targeting and data systems can be integrated to address different vulnerability dimensions, including food insecurity, poverty, and nutrition.The analysis will generate evidence to inform ongoing operational and programmatic collaboration between WFP and relevant ministries, including on economic empowerment, smallholder farmer resilience, school feeding programme, and the 1,000 Days top-up under the Takaful and Karama programme. This evidence will include analysis of the prevalence of food insecurity across activities and target groups, disaggregated by household characteristics, geography, poverty status, and participation in social protection and food assistance programmes. The analysis will draw on social assistance and support reported by surveyed households, as well as reported income sources and economic sectors.In addition, the consultant will support the strengthening of national institutions’ capacity to analyze and monitor food security and nutrition indicators. This will enhance the ability of national institutions to generate timely, policy relevant evidence and inform government decision making on food security and nutrition. The analysis will inform policy dialogue and programmatic evidence generation on food security and nutrition with the government and will be reflected in inter-agency collaboration. This includes contributions to the Country Analysis and UNSDCF, food security working groups, roundtable discussions, and engagement with partner agencies.Collaboration will be maintained with the VAM Officer in Egypt CO and senior consultants in HQ to ensure robust evidence generation and policy relevance.

Key Responsibilities of the Programme Policy Officer

1. Data, Analysis and Simulation

  • Analyse Data: Utilize the latest Household Income, Expenditure, and Consumption Surveys (HIECS), ensuring comparability with earlier rounds. Manage and analyze data, incorporating additional relevant surveys and secondary sources as needed.
  • Conduct Simulation Models: Develop and run simulation models to assess the impact of recent and potential economic measures such as currency devaluation, inflation, and subsidy reforms and potential future changes, on food security and nutrition status.
  • Evaluate Impact: Analyse how economic changes affect purchasing power, food security and nutrition status of households based on latest WFP approach and guidelines.
  • Assess overlap: Examine the degree to which food insecurity intersects with poverty, and evaluate the impact of single and multi‑layered social protection and subsidy interventions in addressing household vulnerabilities.

2. Technical Guidance and Capacity Strengthening

  • Contribute to the capacity strengthening of national institutions in the analysis and monitoring of food security and nutrition related indicators, to mainstream food security and nutrition component, in alignment with government priority interventions under the food system agenda.
  • Provide Technical Guidance: Offer expert advice on the surveys, context, simulation modelling techniques and methodologies.
  • Formulate Strategic and Operational Advice: Deliver strategic and operational recommendations based on analytical findings.
  • Support the introduction and implementation of nutrition analyses and data tools including the Cost of Diet and Cost of Food and Nutrition.

3. Report Preparation including:

  • Methodology: Document methodologies used, including simulation models and data sources.
  • Analysis: Present detailed findings from simulations, including poverty–food insecurity overlaps and implications for nutrition outcomes.
  • Findings: Provide a comprehensive summary of results, including any potential scenarios and their impacts.
  • Recommendations: Offer actionable recommendations for policymakers or relevant stakeholders based on the analysis.
