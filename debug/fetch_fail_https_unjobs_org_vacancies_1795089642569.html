Background

The islands of Bougainville are an integral yet autonomous region of Papua New Guinea (PNG). In 2001, the Bougainville Peace Agreement (BPA) was signed between the national Government of Papua New Guinea (GoPNG) and leaders representing the people of Bougainville. The BPA marked the end of a decade-long civil conflict in which up to 20,000 men and women died and many more were left traumatized, without family, access to basic services, education and infrastructure. The UN has supported peacebuilding and post-conflict transition in Bougainville since the establishment in 1998 of the UN Political Office in Bougainville (UNPOB). The subsequent Observer Mission for Bougainville (UNOMB) facilitated and monitored the three key tenets of the BPA: a referendum on the independence of Bougainville, weapons disposal and the establishment of good governance, and the transfer of powers and functions (generally referred to as the ‘draw down’ of powers).

Autonomy arrangements came into force in 2001, and the first Autonomous Bougainville Government (ABG) was established in 2005. Following this, the non-binding Bougainville Referendum was held in 2019, in which 97.7% of the population of the region voted for independence. Subsequent agreements and documents, including the 2021 Sharp Agreement and Wabag Road Map took steps to outline the process of the transfer of functions and powers to Bougainville ahead of the 2025-2027 timeline for Independence.

The BPA stipulates that following the referendum, the two parties to the agreement will consult over its results, with the outcome subject to ratification by the National Parliament. Following several rounds of post-referendum consultations, a Joint Supervisory Body (JSB) resolution was signed in 2024 which agreed the engagement of an independent Moderator to support the Government of PNG and ABG in their consultations. In September 2024, Prime Minister Hon. James Marape and President Hon. Ishmael Toroama requested the UN to support engagement of a Moderator and establishment of an independent Secretariat to support the Moderator’s programme. The Moderator supported consultations which led to the signing of the Melanesian Agreement in June 2025, a key milestone in the Bougainville Peace Process which outlines a pathway forward in accordance with both the national Constitution and the Bougainville Peace Agreement. The Melanesian Agreement reflects a shared commitment to a close, peaceful, and enduring relationship, and commits the parties to exploring the creation of a ‘Melanesian Framework’ with agreed timelines for a pathway forward. The Agreement also calls for UN support with monitoring and implementation to ensure progress and sustained commitment. In turn, in 2026, the UN provided a package of support based on an Independent Facilitator Consultation Mechanism to support third party facilitation of continued dialogue between the parties.

Twenty-five years after the signing of the BPA, the Autonomous Region of Bougainville is entering a decisive, uncertain and time-sensitive phase of its post-conflict transition. The referendum results and outcomes from joint consultations are due to be submitted to Papua New Guinea’s Parliament in late 2026, and Bougainville has identified September 2027 as a proposed political milestone to announce future arrangements regarding its self-governance. The ongoing political process therefore represents a narrow but critical window for proactive engagement for sustaining peace and reinforcing reconciliation to secure the foundations of a prosperous future for the region.

In Bougainville, development, peace, and security expectations remain high yet understanding of possible future scenarios varies across communities. To sustain peace during this period, UN interventions must therefore address the risk that uncertainty around the political process, combined with limited access to accurate and timely information, misinformation/disinformation, unresolved grievances and trauma, and uneven institutional readiness, could erode confidence and trigger localized or wider instability.

In recent years, UNDP programming has sought to meetBougainvillean aspirations of peacebuilding and reconciliation, ensuring peace dividends and longer-term socio-economic recovery. A dynamic range of UNDP projects include flagship Innovation Hubs to pave the way for Bougainville women, youth and innovators to create employment and entrepreneurial opportunities by providing improved access to business development skills and services; support to gender transformative peacebuilding through joint programming on Women, Peace and Security; parliamentary strengthening support to the Bougainville House of Representatives, including dedicated support to the joint parliamentary Women’s Caucus of PNG; support to civil society organisations; and rollout of renewable solar system for communities in South Bougainville to build resilience through expansion of renewable energy access. This area-based and multi-sectoral approach to development reflects the need for holistic and well-integrated programming to maximize development opportunities in Bougainville, while strengthening governance, service delivery and oversight institutions.

In the emerging context outlined above, UNDP wishes to enhance the technical capacities, local ownership and coordination of its governance and peacebuilding programming support at a critical juncture in Bougainville’s peace and development journey. As part of a new UN Peacebuilding Fund joint project ‘Sustaining Peace for Bougainville’s Future’, convened by UNDP, a Programme Coordinator is sought to drive forward work on local peacebuilding, dialogue and reconciliation, and institution-building, as part of broader management of area-based programming in Bougainville.

Position Purpose

To launch the new Sustaining Peace joint project, UNDP is seeking a Programme Coordinator who will work directly with the participating UN organizations (PUNO) and in close coordination with ABG and GoPNG (including the National Coordination Office for Bougainville Affairs, NCOBA) to ensure the successful implementation of the project. The Programme Coordinator will serve as technical lead on governance, peacebuilding and post-conflict transition in Bougainville, overseeing conflict and political economy analysis and ensuring the quality of UNDP technical support on local peacebuilding, strategic communications, public sector reform and community development (including livelihoods).

The Programme Coordinator will work to establish effective implementation of joint project activities to ensure technical coherence and greater impact. The position will support the translation of strategic and programmatic decisions taken by the Programme Steering Committee (PSC) to the planning of activities while also advising the PSC on potential implications of those decisions practically and strategically. The Programme Coordinator will lead the joint project management team of all PUNOs of Sustaining Peace to facilitate and be closely involved in the joint planning, monitoring, coordination and reporting of work plan activities to optimize cooperation, co-creation, mutual support, economies of scale and complementarity of joint action (including site selection, phasing of activities) based on the comparative advantages of the various UN agencies for maximum impact.

Alongside management of the Sustaining Peace joint project, the Programme Coordinator will oversee broader UNDP programmatic interventions in Bougainville, working in close coordination with the Autonomous Bougainville Government and local civil society organisations to ensure an integrated area-based approach. The position will be closely involved in advising the implementation of multisectoral activities (including UNDP’s governance, gender equality, economic transformation and environment programming) in Bougainville. The incumbent will work closely with internal and external counterparts, including Government, national institutions, civil society, private sector etc, to ensure successful delivery of the UN and UNDP programmes at the local level.

The Programme Coordinator will report directly to the Assistant Resident Representative (Governance, Peace and Gender), while working under the strategic guidance of the UNDP Resident Representative and Deputy Resident Representative. The incumbent will work in consultation with the Office of the UN Resident Coordinator and Buka-based Liaison Officer of the Department for Political and Peacebuilding Affairs. The position will be based at the UN’s Joint Field Office in Buka (Bougainville).

The Programme Coordinator will act as the head of UN/UNDP Field office in Buka (Bougainville), while providing support and oversight of the UNDP’s projects with activities operating in the region. The Programme Coordinator supervises relevant project staff working in Bougainville, ensuring cross-unit cooperation and coordination, while working closely with the UNDP Operations Manager on the operational management of the Buka Office.

UNDP adopts a portfolio approach to accommodate changing business needs and leverage linkages across interventions to achieve its strategic goals. Therefore, UNDP personnel are expected to work across units, functions, teams, and projects in multidisciplinary teams in order to enhance and enable horizontal collaboration.
