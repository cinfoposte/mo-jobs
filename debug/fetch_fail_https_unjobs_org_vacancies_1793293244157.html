The fundamental mission of UNICEF is to promote the rights of every child, everywhere, in everything the organization does — in Programmes, in advocacy and in operations. The equity strategy, emphasizing the most disadvantaged and excluded children and families, translates this commitment to children’s rights into action.

How can you make a difference?

Sudan is currently operating in a large-scale L3 emergency context following the outbreak of conflict in April 2023, which triggered widespread displacement and a sharp escalation in grave violations against children. The operating environment remains highly volatile, particularly across Darfur and Kordofan, requiring a response that simultaneously delivers life-saving humanitarian assistance while sustaining systems across the humanitarian–development–peace nexus.

UNICEF’s programme response is centered on child protection priorities, including mental health and psychosocial support, gender-based violence prevention and response, support to unaccompanied and separated children, and explosive ordnance risk education, underpinned by the Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism on grave violations.

Within this context, the Deputy Representative Programmes plays a critical leadership role in driving programme strategy, coherence, and delivery at scale. The role is responsible for ensuring that programme priorities are effectively translated into results in a highly constrained and rapidly evolving environment, strengthening integration across sectors, and maintaining alignment between humanitarian response and longer-term systems strengthening. As a member of the senior management team, the Deputy Representative Programmes supports overall office leadership, contributes to strategic decision-making, and ensures that programme delivery is riskinformed, results-focused, and responsive to the complex operational realities on the ground.

The Deputy Representative supports the Representative in leading and leveraging partnerships with both public and private sectors for the achievement of results and fulfilment of the rights of children. In fragile and humanitarian settings, the Deputy Representative is accountable for the delivery of a timely, efficient and principled humanitarian programmes, in accordance with UNICEF global Policy and Framework for humanitarian action.

Brief country context:

Sudan is one of the largest countries in Africa, with an estimated population of around 42 million, approximately half of whom are children. Prolonged political instability, coupled with the absence of a fully functioning government, has contributed to widespread insecurity and culminated in large-scale conflict since April 2023, affecting most states, including the capital, Khartoum. This has led to a severe deterioration of both the economic and humanitarian situation, with an estimated 15.8 million people, roughly one-third of the population—requiring humanitarian assistance.

These overlapping crises have significantly worsened conditions for children. Mortality rates remain high, with approximately one in 18 children not surviving to their fifth birthday. Those who do survive often face multiple deprivations, including malnutrition, disease, limited access to safe water and sanitation, inadequate protection systems, and disrupted access to education. Even prior to the escalation of conflict, an estimated 6.9 million children aged 6 to 18 were out of school, a figure that has likely increased further due to displacement and insecurity.

Climate-related shocks continue to compound vulnerabilities, with recurrent flooding and drought-like conditions contributing to water shortages and food insecurity. At the same time, outbreaks of endemic diseases such as malaria, cholera, and dengue fever remain widespread across multiple states.

Sudan is also a situation of concern under the UN Security Council agenda on Children and Armed Conflict, requiring systematic monitoring and reporting of grave violations against children. The UN operates within an integrated mission setting, with the UN Country Team led by a Deputy Special Representative of the Secretary-General/Resident Coordinator/Humanitarian Coordinator (DSRSG/RC/HC), alongside the United Nations Integrated Transition Assistance Mission in Sudan (UNITAMS).
