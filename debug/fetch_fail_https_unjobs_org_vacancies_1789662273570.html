Background:

UN Women, grounded in the vision of equality enshrined in the Charter of the United Nations, works for the elimination of discrimination against women and girls; the empowerment of women; and the achievement of equality between women and men as partners and beneficiaries of development, human rights, humanitarian action and peace and security.

Violence against women and girls (VAWG) remains one of the most pervasive human rights violations globally. Approximately one in three women worldwide, or around 840 [1]million women, have experienced physical and/or sexual violence by an intimate partner or non-partner at least once in their lifetime. Progress in preventing and eliminating VAWG remains slow, underscoring the need for sustained investment in prevention, effective laws and policies, survivor-centred services, accountability, evidence generation and strong women's rights movements and civil society action.

The challenge is particularly significant in Eastern and Southern Africa (ESA), where women and girls continue to experience multiple and intersecting forms of violence, including intimate partner and non-partner violence, child marriage, female genital mutilation (FGM) and other harmful practices. [2] Approximately 32 per cent of women aged 20-24 in Eastern and Southern Africa were married or entered into a union before age 18, while FGM remains highly prevalent in several countries in the region. Under-reporting of violence, discriminatory social norms, gaps in implementation and enforcement of laws and policies, limited access to quality survivor-centred services, insufficient financing, weak accountability mechanisms and restrictions on civic space continue to constrain progress.

At the same time, women's rights organizations (WROs), women-led organizations, feminist movements and women human rights defenders play a critical role in challenging discriminatory norms, advocating for policy and legislative reform, generating and using evidence, holding duty bearers accountable and sustaining collective action for women's rights. Strengthening the capacity, resilience, connectivity and collective influence of these actors is therefore central to creating an enabling environment for sustainable progress on EVAW.

Within this context, UN Women, with financial support from the European Union, implemented the Advocacy, Coalition Building and Transformative Feminist Action to End Violence Against Women (ACT) Programme. ACT was designed to strengthen feminist women's rights organizations and movements as critical actors in accelerating progress towards ending violence against women and girls.

The ACT Programme adopted a movement-strengthening approach that went beyond supporting individual activities or organizations. It invested in organizational systems, feminist leadership, coalition building, relationships, evidence generation, collective advocacy and engagement with policy and accountability processes. In the Eastern and Southern Africa region, the programme worked with regional and national women's rights organizations, feminist networks, youth-led organizations, organizations representing women and girls with disabilities, research institutions, community-based organizations, survivor-led initiatives and other partners.

The ESARO component was structured around two interrelated programme outcomes: strengthened coalition building, networking, leadership and resilience of regional feminist women's rights movements; and increased and enhanced advocacy, campaigning and policymaking on EVAW through multistakeholder partnerships and women's rights coalitions.

Across the three-year implementation period, ACT investments contributed to organizational strengthening and resilience, feminist leadership, networking and coalition building, evidence generation and knowledge use, collective advocacy, policy engagement and accountability. Programme documentation indicates that partners strengthened organizational systems and capacities, developed stronger relationships and collective advocacy platforms, increased engagement of diverse constituencies, generated locally led evidence and expanded participation in national, regional and global policy spaces. ACT also supports regional organizations and coalitions to generate and exchange evidence and practice-based knowledge to inform advocacy, research, policy, programming and funding decisions on EVAW.

As the ACT Programme concludes, UN Women ESARO requires a comprehensive Final Programme Report covering the full three-year implementation period. The report will consolidate and synthesize the results documented through partner final reports and other programme evidence, including programme monitoring and reporting data, case studies, learning products, mission reports, advocacy documentation and other relevant materials.

The Final Programme Report will provide a coherent account of the programme's implementation journey, achievements, significant changes and contributions, while documenting the challenges encountered, adaptations made, lessons learned, good practices and areas that should be sustained or considered in future programming.

The report is intended as a key programme accountability, learning and knowledge product and will be shared with the European Union, as the programme's donor, as well as relevant UN Women stakeholders. It must therefore meet a high standard of analytical quality, accuracy, consistency and presentation and should communicate the programme's results in a clear, compelling and accessible manner.

The consultant will be reporting to the ACT Regional Coordinator Ending Violence Against Women and Girls, and will be supported by the Finance & Programme Associate, who will be the point of contact on the contract and payment issues.

Purpose of the consultancy

The purpose of this consultancy is to develop a comprehensive, evidence-based and donor-facing Final ACT Programme Report covering the full three-year implementation period of the programme.

The consultant will synthesize three years of programme implementation and partner-level evidence into one coherent, evidence-based and donor-facing Final ACT Programme Report for submission to and sharing with the European Union.

The report will consolidate evidence from partner final reports and other relevant programme documentation to demonstrate the programme's achievements and results, highlight significant changes and illustrative impact stories, and capture the key lessons, challenges, adaptations, good practices and sustainability considerations emerging from programme implementation.

The assignment is not an independent programme evaluation. The consultant will not be expected to undertake a separate evaluation of the programme or establish causal attribution. Rather, the consultant will synthesize, analyze and communicate evidence generated through programme implementation and reporting.

Organizational Setting, Reporting and Collaborators

The consultant will work under the overall supervision of the ACT ESARO Programme Coordinator or designated programme lead. The consultant will work closely with relevant ACT ESARO programme personnel and as required, designated monitoring, evaluation, communications, operations and finance colleagues. The designated UN Women focal point will coordinate access to source documentation, consolidate comments on deliverables and serve as the primary contact for contractual and payment matters. Where clarification is required, the consultant may also engage selected programme partner focal points through consultations facilitated or agreed by UN Women.

Description of Responsibilities/ Scope of Work

The consultant will be responsible for producing a coherent suite of ACT ESARO final reporting products covering the full implementation period including supporting annexes: (i) a comprehensive Final Project Report; (ii) an abridged Final Project Report for senior management, donor and external audiences; and (iii) a concise PowerPoint presentation of the programme's headline results, lessons and legacy (iv) supporting annexes. The full report will serve as the authoritative source for the abridged report and presentation. The consultant will undertake the following tasks:

  • Conduct a structured desk review of all relevant documentation provided by UN Women, including the ACT programme document and results framework, Year 1 and Year 2 annual reports, final reports from all ACT ESARO programme partners, relevant partner progress reports where needed to establish continuity, programme monitoring data, indicator tracking information, workplans, knowledge products, advocacy and communications materials, and validated financial information relevant to the final report;
  • Prepare an inception note setting out the proposed approach, annotated report outline, workplan, source-document inventory and any initial information gaps or inconsistencies requiring clarification;
  • Develop and maintain a results consolidation/evidence matrix that maps cumulative reported achievements to the relevant ACT outcomes, outputs and indicators, identifies the underlying source, distinguishes annual from cumulative results, and supports the prevention of duplication or double counting across annual and partner reports;
  • Analyse and synthesize programme achievements across the implementation period, distinguishing between activities undertaken, outputs delivered, outcome-level changes and broader contribution to ACT objectives, to the extent supported by available evidence;
  • Synthesize the contribution of individual programme partners while presenting a coherent regional story of how partner interventions complemented one another and contributed to coalition building, feminist movement strengthening, advocacy, campaigning, policy influence and other ACT results;
  • Identify and clearly articulate significant programme adaptations, implementation challenges, risks and mitigation measures, including contextual factors that affected delivery and how the programme responded;
  • Identify and document good practices, innovations, lessons learned, sustainability considerations and recommendations relevant to future programming and engagement with feminist and women's rights movements;
  • Where necessary, conduct a limited number of virtual consultations with designated UN Women colleagues and/or selected partner focal points to validate interpretation of source material, clarify information gaps and strengthen the accuracy of the report. These consultations will not constitute primary research or an independent evaluation;
  • Prepare a complete first draft Final Project Report including annexes, incorporating a concise executive summary, programme context and approach, implementation overview, results by outcome/output, partner contributions, cross-cutting themes, challenges and adaptations, lessons and good practices, sustainability considerations, conclusions, recommendations, a validated financial overview where required, and relevant annexes;
  • Ensure the report is written in clear, concise and professional English, uses consistent terminology, presents evidence in a balanced and accessible manner, and complies with applicable UN Women and donor reporting requirements and editorial guidance communicated for the assignment;
  • Revise and finalize the full Final Project Report following consolidated comments from UN Women, including up to two substantive review rounds, and provide a final clean, copy-edited and internally consistent version together with the final evidence/results matrix;
  • Following substantive approval of the full Final Project Report, prepare an abridged Final Project Report of approximately 8-12 pages, excluding cover and end matter, as a standalone product for senior management, donor, partner and external audiences. The abridged report should distil the programme's most significant achievements, evidence of change, partner contributions, key lessons, sustainability considerations and selected case examples or headline statistics without merely reproducing the executive summary of the full report;
  • Prepare a concise PowerPoint presentation, excluding title and closing slides, presenting the programme overview, headline results, evidence of change, partner contribution, key lessons, sustainability and legacy. The presentation should be suitable for senior management, donor and stakeholder briefings and should include brief speaker notes or key messages where useful;
  • Ensure that the full report, abridged report and PowerPoint presentation are derived from one agreed results architecture and evidence base and are fully consistent in their use of figures, results statements, terminology and key messages. UN Women will provide applicable branding or presentation templates; advanced graphic design is not required unless separately agreed;
  • Identify significant results and illustrative examples that demonstrate how ACT investments translated into changes for organizations, networks, movements, advocacy processes and policy engagement;
  • Develop selected impact/significant-change stories and case studies based on available evidence and, where necessary, targeted consultations with selected partners or UN Women staff;
  • Develop a coherent narrative of the programme's evolution and major milestones over the three-year implementation period and Synthesize lessons learned and good practices that have potential relevance for future feminist movement-strengthening and EVAW programming;
  • Present the final report and key programme achievements, lessons and legacy to UN Women ESARO.

Approach and Methodology

The consultancy will use a documentary review, evidence synthesis, and analytical report-writing approach.

The consultant will primarily rely on existing programme evidence and will not be required to undertake an independent evaluation or extensive primary data collection.

The approach should include:

  • systematic review of partner final reports and relevant programme documentation;
  • extraction and consolidation of quantitative results;
  • qualitative thematic analysis of reported achievements, changes, challenges and lessons;
  • synthesis of partner-level evidence against the ACT results framework;
  • identification of significant outcomes and illustrative examples;
  • triangulation of evidence across available sources where possible; and
  • targeted consultations with selected stakeholders only where clarification or additional contextualization is required.

The consultant should clearly distinguish between activities delivered, outputs achieved, outcomes/significant changes reported, and broader programme-level contributions.

Where evidence does not substantiate a direct impact claim, the consultant should use appropriately qualified language such as “contributed to,” “supported,” “enabled,” “strengthened” or “helped to advance.”

The consultant should not introduce unsupported achievements, figures, or impact claims into the final report.

Quality And Reporting Standards

The final report should:

  • Be evidence-based and factually accurate;
  • Present quantitative results consistently across the report;
  • Avoid duplication of partner-level achievements;
  • Clearly distinguish outputs from outcomes and broader changes;
  • Synthesize rather than reproduce partner reports;
  • Demonstrate linkages between partner achievements and programme-level results;
  • Use concise and accessible language appropriate for a donor audience;
  • Incorporate the perspectives and voices of programme partners where appropriately documented;
  • Include compelling but substantiated impact/significant-change stories;
  • Identify both achievements and implementation challenges;
  • Provide balanced treatment of successes, limitations and lessons;
  • Maintain consistency with the approved ACT Programme Document and results framework; and
  • Be suitable for external sharing with the European Union.
