Purpose of consultancy:

In collaboration with the Ministry of Women’s Affairs as the lead for the Domestic Violence Law amendment process, UNFPA Cambodia Country Office requests the services of one International Legal Expert to review and propose amendments to the Prevention of Domestic Violence and the Protection of Victims (Domestic Violence Law) passed in 2005.

The International Legal Expert is expected to technically supervise and work in tandem with the National Legal Expert to align their methodologies and ensure cohesive deliverables.

This consultancy is part of the “Strengthening Country Systems for Prevention and Response to GBV in Cambodia" project between MoWA and UNFPA Cambodia Country Office with funding from the Asian Development Bank. This project has the following outputs: (i) governance systems for integrated GBV response strengthened; (ii) institutional capacity for localized GBV response services enhanced; and (iii) GBV prevention models promoted, digital solutions leveraged, and knowledge and lessons learned shared.

Scope of work:

Background and Rationale

In 2005, Cambodia passed the Law on the Prevention of Domestic Violence and the Protection of Victims (Domestic Violence Law). The Committee on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women has recommended comprehensive amendments to this legislation to explicitly define, prohibit, and criminalize all forms of gender-based violence (GBV) and to ensure victims have access to protection orders and effective remedies. The Royal Government of Cambodia has expressed its commitment by supporting several Universal Periodic Review (UPR) recommendations on the amendment of the Domestic Violence Law in the fourth cycle of UPR review of Cambodia.[1]

[1] Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights. (2024). Universal Periodic Review - Cambodia. https://www.ohchr.org/en/hr-bodies/upr/kh-index

There is also a need to update the legislation to align with broader national legal frameworks—including the Criminal Code and Civil Code—and to explore what aspects of Technology-Facilitated Gender-Based Violence (TFGBV) can be incorporated into the amended DV Law as it intersects and amplifies different forms of domestic violence. This reform is critical, as gender-based violence against women and girls remains pervasive; according to the 2021-2022 Cambodia Demographic and Health Survey (CDHS), 21% of ever-partnered women aged 15 to 49 have experienced emotional, physical, or sexual violence from an intimate partner in their lifetime.

The Ministry of Women’s Affairs continues to prioritize the review and reform of the Domestic Violence Law, a key objective embedded across successive iterations of the National Action Plan to Prevent Violence Against Women, including the current NAPVAW IV (2025-2030).

International Legal Expert

Based on the agreed priorities, develop recommendations with detailed explanatory notes to amend the law’s provisions, ensuring strict alignment with international human rights standards, CEDAW recommendations, and best practices.

  • Develop a detailed workplan synchronized with the National Legal Expert.
  • Provide technical advisory support to the Ministry of Women’s Affairs (MoWA) and the Multi-Sectoral Working Group that will be convened to lead the DV Law amendment process. This shall include but not limited to reviewing and providing technical inputs to the Terms of Reference and workplan of the Multi-Sectoral Working Group developed by the national legal expert.
  • Build on existing comprehensive article-by-article legal analysis and develop the Rationale for amending the 2005 Prevention of Domestic Violence and the Protection of Victims (Domestic Violence Law), establishing the human rights imperative and global best practices for why the DV Law needs to be revised. Utilize the trend analysis report from the National Legal Expert to inform the development of this rationale document.
  • Specific to technology-facilitated gender-based violence
    • Incorporate new amendments related to TFGBV or online violence protections that adhere to the Guiding Principles for Law and Policy Reform to address TFGBV.
  • Provide technical inputs to the primary consultations plan from the National Legal Expert. This primary consultation design will include information on the approach, methodology and proposed list of agencies/organizations/persons to be consulted.
  • Analyze the primary consultations report that will be submitted by the national legal expert based on the consultations with relevant government ministries, national and local authorities and civil society organizations who have direct experience in implementing the DV Law.
  • Lead the development of the recommendations for amendments to the 2005 DV Law (‘zero draft’) by applying evidence-based methodologies (e.g., ROCCIPI-Rule, Opportunity, Capacity, Communication, Interest, Process, Ideas and Values) with detailed explanatory notes for all proposed changes (tabular format). This will include (i) the relevant provisions of the existing Domestic Violence Law; (ii) related provisions of other legislation; (iii) gaps, overlaps or inconsistencies; (iv) the proposed amendments, and (v) consequential legislative or regulatory changes required.
  • Co-present the zero draft to the Ministry of Women’s Affairs core team and then to the Multi-Sectoral Working Group with the National Legal Expert.
  • Provide technical inputs to the consultation plan for national and sub-national consultations including with women and girls and community members that will be developed by the national legal expert.
  • Prepare consultation materials including presentation deck.
  • Co-facilitate selected consultations together with the National Legal Expert.
  • Analyze the national and sub-national consultations report from the National Legal Expert.
  • Review the inputs gathered from the public consultations to ensure that integrating this feedback still maintains alignment with international human rights standards then revise the zero draft accordingly.
  • Share the revised zero draft to MoWA core team and then to the Multi-Sectoral Working Group for review and inputs.
  • After two rounds of feedback, submit the updated draft to MoWA and the Multi-Sectoral Working Group for submission to the Council of Ministers.
  • Develop the final report of the DV Law amendment process.

Required expertise, qualifications and competencies, including language requirements:

The International Legal Expert must possess a proven track record in legislative assessment, policy formulation, and statutory drafting within complex legal environments, specifically meeting the following criteria:

  • Advanced university degree (Master's or higher) in Law, Human Rights, or other related fields.
  • At least 7 years of progressive professional experience in conducting comparative legal reviews, legal framework templates, and structural amendment roadmaps based on global evidence in areas related to gender based violence.
  • Demonstrated practical expertise in utilizing the ROCCIPI legislative analysis framework.
  • Deep understanding of international human rights standards and global best practices, CEDAW provisions, specifically CEDAW General Recommendation 35, comparative legal frameworks and global models for domestic violence and intimate partner violence.
  • Demonstrated expertise in designing statutory responses to technology-facilitated GBV is an asset.
  • International experience in domestic violence / GBV law.

Languages:
