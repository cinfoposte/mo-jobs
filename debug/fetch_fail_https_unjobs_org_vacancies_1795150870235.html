VPU Context

The GEF is the world’s largest multilateral fund for the environment. Its family of funds works together to address the planet’s most pressing challenges in an integrated way, helping developing countries meet international environmental goals. Over the past three decades, the GEF has provided more than $28 billion in financing, primarily as grants, and mobilized another $157 billion for country-driven priority projects.

As an independently operating organization, the GEF serves as a financial mechanism for six major international environmental conventions: the Minamata Convention on Mercury, the Stockholm Convention on Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs), the United Nations Convention on Biological Diversity (UNCBD), the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD), the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC), and the High Seas Treaty (BBNJ).

The GEF supports developing countries’ work to address the world’s most pressing environmental issues. The work is organized around five focal areas: biodiversity loss, chemicals and waste, climate change, international waters, and land degradation. With an integrated approach, the GEF’s mission is to strengthen sustainable food systems, forest management, and cleaner cities. The integrated and cross-cutting approach is rooted in the GEF’s commitment to strong engagement with the private sector, Indigenous Peoples, and civil society, as well as to gender equality. The GEF’s operations are implemented through a partnership of 18 implementing agencies.

The GEF is governed by a Council of 32 Country Members representing 186 countries. The GEF Secretariat is supported administratively by the World Bank and operates in a functionally independent and effective manner. The GEF receives scientific and technical advice from the Scientific and Technical Advisory Panel.

Resources for the GEF Trust Fund, which finances the GEF’s programs and projects, are mobilized every four years. As of November 2026, an initial replenishment of $4.4 billion for the ninth replenishment cycle (GEF-9) has been secured with a set of agreed, ambitious programming and policy priorities aimed at further enhancing the GEF’s impact on the global environment. The 4-year GEF-9 cycle for the period 2026-2030 began on 1 July 2026 and is currently being rolled out to recipient countries. Additionally, the GEF continues to implement close to 2,600 active projects and programs from previous cycles.

The GEF is well positioned to continue to be the partner of choice of environmental multilateral agreements, being responsive to its expanding mandate, and facilitating GEF-9 implementation. Presently, the GEF Secretariat comprises seven functional Divisions: Programming; Integration and Knowledge; Partnerships; Conventions and Funds; Operations and Policy; General Management; and Communications. The position of Director, Strategy and Operations, is a foundational part of the GEF’s management structure, responsible for running the day-to-day operations of the Secretariat so that the CEO can focus on strategic direction, external relations, resource mobilization, and representing the GEF.

Duties and accountabilities:

The GEF Secretariat, construed for administrative purposes as a Vice Presidential Unit (VPU) within the World Bank, has approximately 100 professional, administrative and client support staff. The Director of Strategy and Operations will work directly with and report to the CEO. As a core member of the GEF’s Front Office, the Director, Strategy and Operations will serve as the Secretariat’s Chief Operating Officer, supervising the Divisions’ work on behalf of the CEO.

The Director will also play a critical role in delivering the CEO’s vision for GEF 2030: to integrate environmental solutions and development priorities, catalyze finance at scale, and deliver country-owned results with global impact. The Director will have delegated responsibility for the overall operations and internal workings of the Secretariat towards increased efficiency, effectiveness, and impact, and will act as the CEO’s key partner, enabling the CEO to focus on strategy, external relations, resource mobilization, and representing the GEF with key stakeholders.

The Director holds delegated authority for the day-to-day management of the Secretariat, including work programs, workflows, budget execution, staffing, and decisions that fall within approved strategies, policies, and budgets. The CEO retains decision-making authority over key issues, including strategic direction, Council matters, senior appointments, major donor and partner commitments, and exceptions to policy. The Director escalates such matters to the CEO with clear analysis and recommendations, and keeps the CEO informed of significant risks, issues, and decisions taken under delegation. The Director acts on the CEO’s behalf and within the authority delegated by the CEO, and the CEO remains accountable to the Council for the Secretariat’s performance.

Working closely with the CEO and the GEF’s Senior Leadership Team, the Director’s primary responsibilities are as follows:

Strategy

  • Translates the CEO’s vision (Integrate, Catalyze, Deliver) into GEF-wide corporate programming strategies, policies, and priorities, and leads their implementation across the Secretariat, with an increased focus on integration for impact and results
  • Aligns the strategic directions and work programs of all the GEF Divisions with the CEO’s priorities, promoting collaboration and synergies (One GEF) and holding Divisions accountable for delivery
  • Supports the CEO’s strategic outreach to and relationship management with existing and potential donors (including non-sovereign donors) by coordinating the Secretariat’s resource mobilization work, preparing briefings, and ensuring follow-up, including in support of GEF Trust Fund replenishments and the Least Developed Countries Fund (LDCF), the Special Climate Change Fund (SCCF), and the Global Biodiversity Framework Fund (GBFF)
  • Supports the CEO in establishing formalized links and partnerships with the philanthropic community, and manages the resulting follow-up across the Secretariat
  • Supports the CEO in strengthening engagement with the private sector, and ensures the Secretariat has the capacity, processes, and staffing to catalyze finance at scale through blended finance and other non-grant instruments
  • Supports the CEO in building a strategy for enhanced engagement with multilateral development banks, and leads its operational implementation
  • Strengthens the GEF’s institutional relationships and engagement with the GEF implementing agencies through increased portfolio monitoring and oversight, to deliver results faster and with stronger country ownership
  • Increases the efficiency of internal and external GEF operations through simplification of procedures, streamlining, and policy reform, so that results are delivered faster and more cost-effectively
  • Oversees the Secretariat’s technical and operational engagement with international conventions – (i) those for which the GEF serves as financial mechanism, (ii) other nature-related conventions, and (iii) ongoing international environmental negotiations – and supports the CEO’s high-level representation of the GEF in these forums
  • Builds the GEF’s engagement with existing and forthcoming international alliances, initiatives, platforms, and partnerships that are geared towards the Nature Positive goal of 2030
  • Supports the CEO’s dialogue with civil society organizations and Indigenous Peoples, and ensures that gender equality and inclusion are integrated across the Secretariat’s work
  • Promotes and facilitates the use of GEF data and analyses for enhanced, evidence-based decision-making both within the Secretariat and across the Partnership

Operational Management

  • Exercises day-to-day supervision and oversight of the Secretariat’s Divisions on behalf of the CEO, and is accountable to the CEO for their performance
  • Manages the work programs of the GEF Divisions and the workflows among them
  • Assumes primary responsibility for the overall operations and internal workings of the GEF Secretariat, including workflows, budget, systems, and cross-divisional coordination, so that the CEO can focus on strategic direction, external relations, resource mobilization, and representing the GEF
  • Ensures, through the Front Office, that the CEO is fully briefed and supported for high-level engagements with governments, donors, partners, and other key stakeholders, and that commitments made are tracked and followed up across the Secretariat
  • Delivers the programming and policy agenda of GEF-9 as established by the GEF replenishment, faster, at scale, and with strong country ownership, in accordance with the rules, policies and procedures of the GEF Council, tracking progress through portfolio performance data and regular reporting to the CEO
  • Develops multi-year strategic work planning and budgeting, and prepares for the CEO’s oversight the annual budget, programmatic plans, programmatic performance reports, and assessments of the GEF’s efficacy
  • Leads the Secretariat’s preparation for the GEF Council, the LDCF/SCCF Council, and any other Councils that may be established in the GEF’s Family of Funds, ensuring that the CEO, as Chair, is fully briefed for Council meetings and for engagements with Council members and constituencies
  • Prepares, for the CEO, regular reporting to the GEF Council on the progress of the work program, including its pipeline, and to the GEF Assembly on progress toward the GEF-9 Programming Directions and the GEF’s overall strategic goals
  • Coordinates with the World Bank, on behalf of the CEO, the application of all relevant World Bank policies and procedures related to the Secretariat
  • Supports the CEO in overseeing the administration of the funds for which the GEF Secretariat serves as host, including the Adaptation Fund
  • Ensures that the GEF’s business needs are appropriately supported by effective, responsive, and cost-effective technological systems
  • Reports regularly to the CEO on Secretariat performance, risks, and delivery against GEF-9 commitments, and escalates to the CEO issues that require the CEO’s decision

Talent Management

  • Supports the CEO in the redeployment and expansion of staff complements in critical business areas, to ensure that the Secretariat has the right numbers of staff with the right skills that are most effectively deployed to meet current objectives and to ensure a future pipeline of talent
  • Develops, implements, and monitors appropriate strategies for staffing (including consultants and interns), recruitment, training, career development, and skills retraining/enhancement, in accordance with institutional HR practices
  • Models and leads the GEF Leadership Team to create/nurture a culture of collaboration, teamwork, client focus, results orientation, and fairness so that the staff can realize their highest productivity and potential
  • Supervises staff, including through yearly objective-setting and periodic performance reviews
  • Delegates appropriately and holds their staff accountable for delivering agreed work programs based on agreed parameters
  • Demonstrates leadership, with a culture of openness and accessibility to all staff
  • Mentors and motivates staff

Selection Criteria

The successful candidate must demonstrate:

  • Master’s or PhD degree in a relevant field; typically, the successful candidate will have 15-18 years of experience in positions of increasing complexity and responsibility, including direct managerial and supervisory responsibilities
  • A strong knowledge of, and passion for, the GEF’s mission
  • Demonstrated leadership values and exemplification of GEF managerial and core competencies
  • Intellectual leadership, with a track record of applying practice know-how and effective policy dialogue to achieve development results
  • Demonstrated in-depth experience working across thematic and disciplinary boundaries, and with multiple stakeholders – both public and private – in building collaborative alliances for results
  • Proven ability to effectively manage and implement complex change management initiatives
  • Proven track record of running the operations of a complex organization under delegated authority, including budget, human resources, performance management, and results delivery, and of establishing clear accountability and escalation across teams
  • Strong understanding of financial management, including budgeting, financial planning, project control, and driving operational efficiency, and a proven track record of supporting a chief executive in reporting to governing bodies and managing stakeholder relationships on potentially contentious issues
  • Substantive experience in working in and with developing countries
  • Strong written and oral communication skills with proven experience in dealing with external constituencies and expert groups
  • Deep technical knowledge and experience in one or more of the following areas: environmental economics, environmental conservation, political economy, sustainable development, natural resource management, private sector investment, innovative financial instruments, engagement with the philanthropic community
